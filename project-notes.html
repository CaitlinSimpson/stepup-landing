Area 1

Name one specific customer segment or user group: narrower than a sector. Say who they are, who pays or decides (if different), and who uses it every day.

The customer segment selected for this exercise is Irish public sector employees preparing for internal promotions through competency based recruitment. These individuals have practical experience though they struggle to identify and communicate evidence of their achievements against the competencies relevant to the role. They also struggle to match them to the role requirements, identify gaps in their experience, and prepare structured STAR (situation, task, action, result) answers. Then proposed solution is an end-to-end promotion preparation platform that helps employees organise their achievements, identify gaps in their evidence, prepare STAR method responses and practise competency based interview questions. Preparing presentations is also common in. these scenarios and thus, providing support in this area is required. The primary users include individual employees who fall into this remit. Organisations could potenttiallly invest in this tool through learning and development strategies to better enhance their employees skillset within the company.

State the problem in their words, in a line or two (label invented quotes as invented).

‘I have multiple years of experience in various roles, however I struggle to map my examples to the relevant competencies amd turn them into STAR answers when applying for internal promotions. I am the type of person that can execute a task to a very high standard and exceed expectations, though I don’t always translate that well into words. I find it challenging to identify my strangest examples and explain how they align to the compencies for the next grade. My personal goal is to continue to progress within my non-for-profit organisation and thus, I need something that can help me organise my achievements, idetfiy where my evidence lacks detail, and practise translating my experience into strong interview ansers. Ideally, I would like to receive feedback so that I can build and improve on my answers ahead of the interview’.

Let user record, edit, and save their achievements

Area 2

Explain your solution simply: what it is, the kind of technology it is (for example a booking system, a tracker, a portal, a chatbot), and how it works in three steps.

The proposed solution is a website based promotion preparation platform designed for Irish public-sector employees seeking internal promotion. It combines an evidence planner with competency-based interview practice, helping users organise their professional achievements and prepare stronger interview responses.

The platform aims to make promotion preparation more structured by connecting the user's experience to the requirements of their target role.

How it works:

Enter the target role: The user selects or enters their target role and adds the relevant competencies or capabilities from the job description.

Organise professional evidence: The user records examples of their work achievements and maps them to the relevant competencies. The platform highlights competencies for which the user has not yet recorded supporting evidence. Users can then organise selected achievements using the STAR method.

Practise and improve: The user answers competency-based interview questions linked to their target role. They receive feedback on their answers, can make improvements and practise again. Presentation preparation may also be included for roles requiring a presentation.

Say how it is paid for or sustained: a price, a membership fee, a grant or sponsorship, or time saved. Label figures as assumptions.

The proposed business model is based on a freemium approach. Users could access basic evidence-planning and STAR preparation features for free, with a potential paid subscription offering additional interview practice, more detailed feedback and presentation preparation.

An alternative revenue stream could involve organisations purchasing access for employees through their Learning and Development departments.

As an initial assumption for testing, a premium subscription could cost €5 per month per individual. This is a proposed price, not a validated market rate. The viability of this model would depend on user demand, development and operating costs, and willingness to pay.

The main ongoing costs could include hosting, maintenance and any AI services used to provide feedback. The long-term sustainability of the platform would depend on whether subscription income or organisational funding could cover those costs.

Design for the user's real situation (on a phone, at work, in a hurry), in the design itself, not only in words.

Users may prepare for promotion outside working hours or during short breaks, so the platform should be simple to navigate on a mobile phone. Forms should use clear labels, short sections and readable text. Achievement records should be easy to edit, and users should be able to move between competencies without losing their work. Progress and missing evidence should be clearly visible, without requiring users to read long instructions. These requirements will be checked by testing the demo on a phone as well as a laptop.

StepUp is a student prototype. The working demo uses fictional sample data, rule-based feedback and browser-local storage. No paid subscription, presentation-preparation tool or cloud sync is currently offered.
